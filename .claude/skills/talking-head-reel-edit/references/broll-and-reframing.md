# Replacing A-roll: reframes, B-roll, and graphics

A single talking-head take gets monotonous after ~3 s. The reference reel never leaves its one setup
for more than 2 s, yet feels multi-shot. Use these, cheapest first.

## 1. Reframe the A-roll (always)

Record at 4K or crop-friendly 1080p so you can punch in without visible softness.

- **Punch-in on a beat change.** Start a new take at 120–130% and ease back to 100% as its hero word
  lands (`@punch`). Hides the jump cut and adds energy.
- **Alternate framings between jump cuts.** 100% (chest-up) ↔ 115–125% (shoulders-up). Never two
  cuts in a row at the same scale. A same-scale jump cut reads as a mistake.
- **Slow push** (100→108% over 1.5–2 s, `@push`) on a statement that should feel weighty.
- Keep the eyes on the upper-third line at every scale; adjust position along with scale.

## 2. One literal B-roll insert per ~15 s

Put it at the emotional low point (the "agitate" beat) and make it show the line literally: VO says
"being on camera stresses you out", picture shows the person stressed behind a camera. Rules:

- 1.5–2.5 s, VO continues underneath (cutaway, no lip-sync needed).
- Same person, same wardrobe and set if at all possible. Continuity makes it read as "the same
  story" instead of stock footage.
- Exaggerate the emotion (grimace, slump). It is shorthand, not documentary.
- Grade it cooler, darker and less saturated than the A-roll to mark a negative state.
- Cut in hard (no transition: the jolt is the point). Get out with a 5-frame shake (`@shake`) or a hard cut.
- Frame it so the face is centred, and move the caption lockup to the top (`{top}`).
- Wrap it in graphics that make it a "thought": spotlight ring (`@ring`) and inner-voice bubble (`[bubble]`).

### Sources

1. **Shoot it**: 10 minutes after the A-roll, same set: behind the camera, staring at a blank doc,
   phone notifications, and so on. The best option.
2. **Generate it** from a still of the creator with an image-to-video model (the reference reel's
   insert shows Google Gemini/Veo's sparkle watermark and an AI-garbled logo). Only do this with the
   on-camera person's consent. Keep the platform's AI-content label and the generator's watermark or
   disclosure; don't edit them out. Check hands, text on screens and logos for artifacts, and keep it
   under ~2.5 s where artifacts are least noticeable.
3. **Stock** only if nothing else works. It reads generic, so keep it short and graded to match.

## 3. Graphics that recast the A-roll (no new footage)

- **REC viewfinder** (`@rec`) when the topic is filming or recording: the same shot becomes "her view
  of herself recording". Flicker it on; remove it on the solution line.
- **Notification / warning cards** (`[card:…]`) to list doubts or objections: stack 2–3, pop words
  in as spoken.
- **Text behind the subject** (`{behind}`) once per reel, on the key line, with the lockup at the top
  of frame and the hero big.
- **Send icon / tap circle / strike-through** for small beats.

## 4. The state change

Build up the problem graphics (frame + cards) across the agitate beat. Then remove all of them in one
cut on the first word of the solution. Viewers feel the relief as a cleaner frame before they process
the words.
