# Reference reel: frame-by-frame breakdown

The style in this skill was reverse-engineered from one reel: a 16.9 s, 9:16 talking-head ad for a
content agency. A woman in a brown NY cap and denim jacket talks to camera in a dark office set.
This file is the ground truth behind the numbers in `style-spec.md`. Read it when someone asks
how the original was made, or when you need to justify an editing choice.

## 2. Script and structure

VO (as captioned): *"If the prospects have to meet you to understand your value, your content is
failing. You know content matters. Being on camera stresses you out. Even if you record, you are
unsure what to say, how it turn(s) to revenue. We take it off your plate."*

| Beat | Time | Lines | Visual treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0.00–3.10 | "If the Prospects have to meet you / to Understand your value" | Opens punched in, pulls back as the first hero word lands; send icon flies in |
| Problem | 3.13–6.20 | "your Content is failing / You know content matters" | Blur-zoom in, red strike-through; stripe-flash to the one text-behind-head shot |
| Agitate | 6.20–14.77 | "being on Camera Stresses you out / even if You record you are unsure / what to say? how it turn to revenue?" | Only B-roll insert (spotlight ring + inner-voice bubble), shake back to A-roll, REC viewfinder, red warning cards |
| Solution | 14.77–16.90 | "we take it off Your plate" | Every problem graphic disappears on one cut; clean frame, lime payoff word, ends without a CTA card |

The lime hero words alone tell the story: **Prospects → Understand → Content (struck out) →
content → Camera Stresses → You record → [what to say? / revenue?] → Your plate**.

## 4. Caption system

**Lockup anatomy.** Every caption group is a small typographic lockup, not a subtitle line:

```
   lead words            top-right words        <- small white, ~0.44x hero, Inter-Bold-like
 H E R O   W O R D                              <- lime, heavy grotesk, soft lime glow
                    tail words (right-aligned)  <- small white
```

- Hero: one or two words, lime `#E0FD56`, heavy neo-grotesk (Inter Black / SF Pro Heavy look),
  tight tracking, ~68% of frame width (up to ~92% for the "big" moment), soft lime halo.
- Lead words start ~15% in from the hero's left edge, sit on its cap line and tuck into it a little.
- "Top" words (only "have to") sit over the right half of the hero.
- Tail words hang under the hero, right-aligned to its right edge.
- Small words measure 45–90 px on a 1080-wide frame: about 0.44× the hero. Long asides ("you are
  unsure") get smaller still.

**Placement.** Mid-frame (lockup centred ~55% down) on the dark jacket below the face. Top of frame
(~15–20%) when the middle is taken: the behind-the-head moment and the B-roll with the ring.
Never in the bottom quarter, where platform UI sits.

**Timing.**
- Words appear on the frame they are spoken. Small words: 4 f fade up from a blur with a ~12 px rise.
  Hero: 6–8 f land, scale ~118%→100%, blur ~28→0 px, opacity 0→100, fast start and long soft settle.
- The "slam" variant (behind-head "content") appears fully formed in one frame.
- Groups never animate out. They are cut on the next jump cut, so text and picture change together.
- One group is on screen at a time, ~1.4–2.1 s each.

## 6. Transitions and camera

| Move | Where | Build |
|---|---|---|
| Punch-out on the first hero | 0.27 s | Open at ~125%, ease to 100% over 8 f as the hero lands |
| Blur-zoom | 3.13 s | 3 f of blur up + creep to ~110%, cut, land at ~130% blurred, ease to 100% sharp over ~10 f. Blurs the captions too |
| Stripe flash | 4.53 s | ~4 f ramp to near-white with fine dark vertical lines (~70 across the frame), cut at the brightest frame, ~5 f back down |
| Slow push | 4.6–6.2 s | ~100→108%, eased |
| Hard cut into B-roll | 6.2 s | No transition: the bluntness sells the stress |
| Shake out of B-roll | 8.1 s | 5 f horizontal shake (±40 px decaying) with directional blur; captions shake too; hard cut after |
| Hard cut that clears the HUD | 14.77 s | All problem graphics removed in one frame on the solution line |

Budget: roughly one transition every 2–3 s, each type used once. Most cuts are plain jump cuts
hidden by the caption change.

## 7. How the A-roll gets replaced

The reel is one continuous talking-head setup, yet it feels like several shots. Four techniques:

1. **Reframing instead of new angles.** Punch-ins (125–130%) at the start of takes, plus a slow
   push, make jump cuts read as deliberate camera moves.
2. **One literal B-roll insert, 2 s, at the emotional low point.** VO continues ("being on camera
   stresses you out") over a cutaway that acts it out: the same person behind a mic and camera rig,
   grimacing. It is very likely AI-generated:
   - a four-point sparkle in the bottom-right corner matches Google's Gemini/Veo visible watermark;
   - the camera's flip screen shows a garbled cap logo ("IK" instead of "NY"), a typical AI text artifact;
   - the set (abstract painting, plants, leather chair) and wardrobe match the A-roll, as if it was
     generated from a still of the creator.

   It is graded cooler, darker and less saturated than the A-roll to mark a negative state.
3. **Graphics that recast the A-roll.** The REC viewfinder turns the same talking-head shot into
   "what she sees while recording" without new footage. The red cards add visual load on top.
4. **Depth trick.** Masking the subject over the hero word once makes a flat shot feel layered.

## 8. Look (grade)

Low-key office set: charcoal wall, warm skin with soft frontal key, red/teal abstract art and dark
plants as background colour, strong fall-off to near-black below the chest (vignette). The dark
lower half is the caption stage: the lime hero and white words reach maximum contrast there. B-roll
is cooler and more desaturated.

## 9. Audio

- VO only. No music bed: every pause drops to digital silence (≈ −75 dBFS). Pauses were cut or
  gated hard (3.00–3.13, 7.90–8.23, 13.73–14.07, 15.27–15.40, 16.37–16.80 s).
- The only clear SFX is a ~4 kHz double beep at ~9.07–9.25 s, right after the REC frame appears
  (a camera "record" beep). Transitions are silent; the motion carries them.

## 10. Why it holds attention

1. **Motion in the first 300 ms.** It starts mid-sentence on a tight frame with text already on screen;
   the hero lands with a camera pull-back at 0.27 s.
2. **A skim track.** Only one lime word per beat; reading just the lime words gives the whole pitch.
   Small white words give context without competing.
3. **Something changes every ≤1 s**: a word appears, an icon moves, a card pops. Something structural
   (a cut, transition or new graphic) every ~1.5–2 s. The longest static stretch, the cards from
   12.4 to 14.7 s, is carried by her gestures.
4. **Colour means something.** Lime = the idea, red = problem (strike, warning cards, REC), coral =
   inner voice, cream = spotlight. Viewers learn the code within seconds.
5. **Native phone UI.** Send icon, notification pill, camera viewfinder and error toasts are read
   instantly because viewers see them all day.
6. **Problem → relief as a visual state change.** The REC frame and red cards stack up the doubts;
   one cut removes all of them on "we take it off your plate". The clean frame is the payoff.
7. **Each trick used once.** One B-roll, one depth shot, one flash, one shake, one blur-zoom, so none
   wears out in 17 s.
8. **Tight, music-free VO** at ~163 wpm keeps the cadence urgent.

## 11. How this breakdown was made

Repeat this on any new reference before copying its style:

```bash
ffprobe -v error -show_streams reel.mp4                      # size, fps, duration
ffmpeg -i reel.mp4 -vf "select='gt(scene,0.15)',showinfo" -f null - 2>&1 | grep pts_time   # hard cuts
# contact sheet: every frame of a range, numbered (here f0-f23)
ffmpeg -i reel.mp4 -vf "select='between(n,0,23)',scale=240:-1,drawtext=text='f%{n}':x=4:y=4:fontsize=20:fontcolor=yellow,tile=8x3" -frames:v 1 -vsync 0 sheet.png
ffmpeg -i reel.mp4 -lavfi "showspectrumpic=s=1600x500:legend=1" spec.png               # music bed? SFX?
```

Read the sheets range by range (24 frames per sheet). Measure colours and text sizes from full-res
frames (median of the brightest pixels in a box). Note when each element enters and leaves, and how
many frames each animation takes.
